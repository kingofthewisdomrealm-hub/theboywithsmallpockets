Once upon a time, in a quiet little village where people lived small lives and carried small dreams, there was a boy named Joe.
Joe was not poor.
He was not hungry.
And he was not unhappy.
But Joe had one problem.
His pockets were very, very small.
In Joe's village, everyone had pockets just like his.
Each pocket could hold exactly ten gold coins.
No more.
And no less.
The people of the village were proud of this.
"Ten is safe," the elders would say.
"Ten is normal."
"Ten is enough."
So every morning, Joe would wake up, put on his little coat, fill his pockets with ten gold coins, and go about his day.
And for a long time…
Joe never questioned it.
Until one night, something strange happened.
As Joe was walking home, he saw a faint golden light shining beneath an old stone near the edge of the village.
He moved the stone.
And underneath it…
was a map.
Not an ordinary map.
The paper seemed almost alive.
The lines shimmered.
The letters moved.
And at the very center of the map were three words:
MORE THAN ENOUGH.
Joe stared at them.
More than enough?
He had never heard anyone in the village speak those words before.
The map pointed beyond the village walls…
into a place the elders warned everyone never to enter.
The Forest of Too-Much.
They said everything inside the forest was too big.
The trees were too tall.
The fruit was too plentiful.
The roads went too far.
The dreams were too dangerous.
And worst of all…
people who entered the forest sometimes came back different.
Joe looked at his ten gold coins.
Then he looked at the map.
And for the first time in his life…
ten coins did not feel like enough.
So Joe walked into the forest.
At first, everything seemed beautiful.
He saw apples bigger than his head.
Streams filled with sparkling water.
Golden light between the trees.
And strange paths that seemed to go on forever.
But the deeper Joe walked…
the more uncomfortable he became.
His heart beat faster.
His hands began to shake.
And then…
from behind a tree…
something jumped into the road.
It was small.
Ugly.
Loud.
And angry.
The Panic Goblin.
The creature pointed at Joe and screamed:
"GO BACK!"
Joe froze.
The Goblin stepped closer.
"That treasure is not for boys like you!"
Joe looked down at his little pockets.
The Goblin laughed.
"Look at you."
"You can barely hold ten coins."
"What are you going to do with MORE?"
And suddenly…
Joe felt foolish.
Maybe the Goblin was right.
Maybe ten really was enough.
Maybe the village knew something he did not.
Joe turned around.
But before he could take a step…
the ground began to shake.
Boom.
Boom.
Boom.
Something enormous was moving through the forest.
Out from between the trees came a giant.
Joe looked up.
And up.
And up.
The giant had Joe's face.
The same eyes.
The same hair.
The same smile.
But everything about him seemed larger.
Stronger.
Calmer.
Joe whispered:
"Who are you?"
The giant smiled.
"I'm you."
Joe looked confused.
The giant pointed toward the Panic Goblin.
"That creature is not guarding the treasure."
"He is guarding the old Joe."
Joe looked at the Goblin again.
The Goblin suddenly seemed smaller.
The giant leaned down.
"You keep asking how to get more gold."
"That is the wrong question."
Joe swallowed.
"What is the right question?"
The giant smiled.
"Who do you have to become… to hold it?"
Joe looked at his pockets.
For the first time, he realized something.
His pockets were not small.
They were simply…
unstretched.
So Joe kept walking.
Every step through the forest stretched him.
He learned to carry heavier things.
He learned to walk farther.
He learned to stay calm when he was afraid.
He learned to trust himself.
And little by little…
his pockets grew.
Finally, Joe reached the place marked on the map.
There, beneath an enormous ancient tree, sat a golden chest.
Carved across the front were the words:
MORE THAN ENOUGH.
Joe's hands trembled.
He opened the chest.
And inside…
there was no gold.
No jewels.
No crown.
Only a mirror.
Joe stared into it.
At first, he was disappointed.
Then he noticed something.
The boy looking back at him…
was not the same boy who had left the village.
His shoulders were broader.
His eyes were calmer.
And hanging from his coat…
were two enormous pockets.
Joe smiled.
And suddenly he understood.
The gold was never the treasure.
Becoming the boy who could hold it…
was.
Joe returned to the village.
But he did not return carrying mountains of gold.
He returned carrying something more valuable.
Bigger pockets.
The villagers gathered around him.
One child stepped forward.
"Joe…"
"Did you find the treasure?"
Joe smiled.
He reached into one of his enormous pockets…
pulled out a single gold coin…
and placed it in the child's hand.
Then he said:
"It was never about more coins."
"It was about a bigger you."
And from that day forward…
the children of the village stopped asking:
"How do I get more?"
They started asking:
"Who do I need to become… to hold more?"
Because sometimes…
the thing standing between you and everything you want…
is not the size of the opportunity.
It is the size of the pockets you brought with you.
